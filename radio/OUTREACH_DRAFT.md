## English version

**Subject: Request for a one-time student research test transmission (FT8, ~60 seconds)**

Assalam-o-Alaikum and greetings,

My name is Muhammad Imran. I am a 19-year-old BS English student in
Pakistan and an independent student researcher. I am writing to request
the Society's kind help with a single, one-time radio experiment.

For the past months I have been building **Nature-Mirror Mesh**, an
open-source research project on messaging that works when the internet
does not — using Reticulum/LXMF software and nature's own reflectors
(the ionosphere) instead of commercial infrastructure. The software side
is complete and publicly documented, and I have already proven the
*receiving* half using free public receivers abroad. What I cannot do
myself is the *transmitting* half: I hold no amateur license and own no
equipment, and I will not transmit unlawfully.

I therefore respectfully ask: would a licensed member, or the Society's
club station, be willing to transmit **one short test beacon as control
operator, under your own callsign**, at a time entirely of your
choosing? The whole transmission is about **60 seconds**: your normal
FT8 CQ, then two standard FT8 messages carrying a short **plaintext**
check token, then your CQ again. The token and its encoding are
publicly documented; there is no encryption and nothing commercial. I
will monitor PSK Reporter and public receivers myself and share the
complete reception report with you and the Society for your log.

I completely understand if the Society cannot help or if its rules do
not permit this — even guidance would be gratefully received. I am also
starting the proper path in parallel: I wish to join PARS as a Short
Wave Listener and work toward my own PTA license, and any advice on
beginning that process as a student would mean a great deal.

Thank you for your time, and for the Society's service to Pakistan —
especially its emergency work, which is an inspiration for this project.

With respect,
**Muhammad Imran**
BS English student · Student researcher, Nature-Mirror Mesh
(public project record available on request)
